Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants convert light energy into chemical energy. Chlorophyll in the chloroplasts absorbs sunlight, mainly in the red and blue wavelengths. During the light reactions, water molecules are split and oxygen is released as a by-product. The energy captured is stored in ATP and NADPH molecules. In the Calvin cycle, carbon dioxide from the air is fixed into glucose using that stored energy. This glucose feeds the plant and, through the food chain, nearly every other living thing on Earth. Without photosynthesis the oxygen level in the atmosphere would fall dramatically within a few thousand years.
